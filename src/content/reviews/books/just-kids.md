## Monday's Children

- I found it very beautiful how she described her first time seeing a swan:
> A long curving neck rose from a dress of white plumage
>
- The view of the swan awoke a desire inside her to talk about its beauty. She even called its appearance a miracle. This yearning stayed with her even after the swan flew away;
- Patti was born during a great blizzard in 1946. Her mother went into heavy labor inside a taxi. She was born with pneumonia, and her father kept her alive by holding her over the steam that came out of the bathtub;
- She had a sister, Linda, and a brother, Todd. They lived under poor conditions, but even so, their parents tried to give them a good childhood;
- Her mother taught her to pray, and it really bothered her to have to repeat the same words instead of saying what really was on her heart and mind;
> It pleased me to imagine a presence above us, in continual motion, like liquid stars
>
- When she stopped repeating those same memorized words and started to make her own prayers, she saw what her imagination could do. As she stated, she would mouth long letters to God;
> My small torrent of words dissipared into an elaborate sense of expanding and receding. It was my entrance into the raciance of imagination.
>
- She was sick a lot, and she would spend those days bedridden, entertaining herself just using her imagination;
- Her first experience with the idea of other worlds besides ours was through prayers and religion; the second was with books, through her mother's influence;
    - She even hid her mother's books under her pillow to "absorb the meaning";
    - They both would sit on the couch and read together (each with their own book);
- The books opened a whole new world for her. She wanted to read every book ever written. And each book would make her want to experience new things and visit new places;
    - But what she desired the most was to express herself, and she started to do that with the support of her siblings, who would listen to her made-up stories/scenarios, act in her plays, and play whatever game she would make up;
    - [I have this feeling. I sometimes feel bad thinking about all the music, books, and movies that will come out after I'm dead that I won't even know exist. And I also feel overwhelmed thinking about all the works of art that have been released and that I haven't consumed.];
- Stephanie was her friend, who was very sick. Patti admitted that she was more interested in Stephanie's belongings than in her friendship;
    - The last time that Patti saw Stephanie, she stole a pin while Stephanie was asleep and felt immediate guilt. She couldn't sleep at night and swore she would take it back and beg for forgiveness, which she couldn't do because Stephanie died the following day;
- Stephanie's mom gave her stuff to Patti, but Patti couldn't even look at it, covered with guilt;
- Patti constantly told us how poor her childhood was;
- Robert was fascinated by that story;
> I was a bad girl trying to be good and he was a good boy trying to be bad.
>
- Patti's thoughts would often wander when she was in class, which would earn her humiliating punishments;
    - She would draw these moments for Robert:
    > He delighted in them, seeming to appreciate all the qualities that repelled or alienated me from others. Through this visual dialog, my youthful memories became his
    >
- Then, Patti's younger sister was born, Kimberly;
- Despite being poor, her parents seemed to try to give them a good childhood - the father introduced her to sci-fi, for example. She talked about her family with love;
- She started to write stories after reading Little Women and seeing that Jo could support her family through writing;
- Her parents made a huge effort to take all the kids to the museum, and that really had an impact on Patti's life. Not only did her father make the sacrifice to take them there, but he also discussed art with her, talking about what he liked and didn't like;
- After the museum visit, she changed. She saw other people's view of the world and she wanted to express hers also;
    > I wondered if I had been called as an artist. I didn't mind the misery of a vocation, but I dreaded not beeig called
    >
    - Her biggest fear was not being able to express herself through her art - any type of art form;
- In high school, she wasn't popular. According to her, she was on the lowest rung of high school's social ladder. To get through this period of her life, Patti immersed herself in books and rock'n'roll - James Brown, The Shirelles, Hank Ballard and The Midnighters;
- Unlike many other artists I know, she wasn't naturally great at anything, and yet she drew, danced and wrote poems;
> I imagined myself as Frida to Diego, both muse and maker. I dreamed of meeting an artist to love and support and work with side by side
>
    - I have a take on this passage. I think it's beautiful how she wanted someone else to understand her. She wanted to pour all of herself out on someone. This reminds me of the song Oceano by Djavan. She wanted someone who would see all of her, and for her to be all of herself with him;
    - Also, something tells me that she found that with Robert;
- After talking about her childhood, she changed the subject and tried to talk about his;
    - They both were born in the same year, 1946;
    - He was fascinated with beauty since he was born;
    > He contained, even at an early age, a stirring and the desire to stir
    >

    > Colloring excited him, not the act of filling in space, but choosing colors that no one else would select
    >
    - He liked his art to disturb others (initially, his siblings);
        - Both used their siblings/family as their audience for their artistic expressions;
    - He was a natural draftsman, unlike Patti, who, according to her, wasn't naturally good at anything;
    > He was an artist, and he knew it. It was not a childish notion. He merely acknowledged what was his
    >
    - Apparently, he always had a desire to bother people;
        > The trill of the battle between good and evil, attracted him, perhaps because it mirrored his interior conflict, and revealed a line that he might yet need to cross
        >
    - Their family was very different. He liked hearing her stories with her siblings and parents because he didn't have any stories worth telling;
- In 1966, she got pregnant;
- Despite growing up in a Christian household that valued marriage, her parents supported and loved her through that phase;
- She was studying to become a teacher, even though she didn't see herself working with that;
    - Her parents were proud of her studies and work (at a factory). The pregnancy announcement was a great blow;
- The father of her child was younger, and she didn't ask anything of him. The whole responsibility would be hers;
- She couldn't raise the kid well, so she found a good family to take the child in;
- She made an important decision when she was about to tell her parents about her baby: she would go after her dream. No more college or work at a factory. She promised herself to be an artist;
- Janet Hamill was a friend from college, who came to live with her family. She helped Patti throughout the pregnancy;
    - They shared a love for rock music;
- Patti had to leave home, not because of her parents, but thanks to the neighbours, who made their life a living hell;
- She went to live with the family that would adopt her baby;
    - A house full of music and art;
- Her parents gave her support during the birth, but it was very difficult and she was very neglected;
    - The nurses neglected her and left her suffering for hours before calling the doctor;
- She knew that the child would grow up happy, and that gave her strength to go after her dream;
    > Young Jean (of Arc) whom I had known through books and the child whom I would never know. I vowed to both of them that I would make something of myself
    >
- The same day she was giving birth, Robert was also having an intense experience: dropping a large amount of acid;
    - He tried to do that before, but the drugs took him to a dark place in his mind and he didn't stop to think about it; he just avoided his negative thoughts;
- Just like Patti described her labor, Robert described what he felt after taking LSD;
    > He had past through the phase of antecipation and nervous agitation. He loved that feeling. He traced the thrill and fear blossoming in his stomach. He used to experience it as an altar boy as he stood behind the velvet courtains in his small robe holding the processional cross, reading to march
    >
    > He noticed his own breath like the breath of a collapsing god
    >
- The drugs worked that time. He could see a drawing on an empty canvas;
- Both experiences (labor and drugs) brought them the clarity to follow their dream to be an artist;
- In '67, Patti was a college dropout working on a minimum wage job in a textbook factory;
- While Robert was 100% sure of his artistry, Patti still said:
    > I held to the hope that I was an artist
    >
    - She had her parents' support, but not the means to follow that career in rural South Jersey;
- An important influence for her was Arthur Rimbaud, through the book Illuminations (that she stole from a book shop);
- That next quote reminds me of a lyric from Illicit Affairs by Taylor Swift:
    > Rimbald held the keys to a mystical language that I devoured even as I could not fully decipher it
    >
    - Probably she couldn't fully decipher it because of the language barrier, but she surely had a deep connection with his words;
- Rimbaud's words followed her wherever she went;
    > My unrequited love for him was as real to me as anything I had experienced
    >
- They harassed her for reading a foreign book, accusing her of communism;
    > It was for him that I wrote and dreamed. He became my archangel, delivering me from the mundane horrors of factory life. His hands had chiseled a manual of heaven and I held them fast
    >
    > I tossed my copy of Illuminations in a plaid suitcase. We would escape together
    >
- She wanted to be in an environment surrounded by art, so she could learn. That's why she wanted to go to New York;
- She took the opportunity to go when she was laid off;
- The plan was to spend all her money buying a one-way ticket to New York and work in a bookstore;
- Then, she realized that the price of the ticket to NY had almost doubled. She didn't give up on going because she found 32 dollars in a lost purse and took it as a sign;
    > I can only thank, as I have within myself many times through the years, this unknown benefector
    >
    > I accepted the grant of the small white purse as the hand of fate pushing me on
    >
- This was her chance to make her break;
    > No one expected me. Everything awaited me
    >
- She hoped to crash in her friend's apartment. They had moved;
- She spent a long time without a place to stay, without food. She spent all her time looking for a job;
    - As time passed, she got more beat up and hungrier;
- Saint was also a homeless person who helped her with food, conversations, and advice;
    > -- Hey sister, what's your situation?
    > -- On Earth or in the universe?
    >

    > -- A real prision breakfast -- I said
    > -- Yeah, but at least we are free
    > And that summed it up
    >
- When Saint left, she said:
    > I wasn't sad, because everythime I though of him I'd smile
    >
    - It is better to have loved and lost than to not have loved at all;
- John Coltrane's death was a huge deal for her (and other Americans);
    > It was if a saint had died, one who had offered up healing musiv yet was not permitted to heal himself
    >
- She still didn't have a job nor a place to sleep:
    > That wasn't much fun, but I had my mantra: "I'm free, I'm free". Although after several days, my other mantra:"I'm hungry, I'm hungry" seemed to be in the forefront
    >
- Finally, Patti and Robert met;
- (I just realized that maybe the chapter's name - Monday's Children - is because they both were born on Monday. Given that she is superstitious and thinks that Mondays are a lucky day, she must've felt like Robert and her were meant to meet);
